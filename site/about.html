How it works

A prototype that asks one question: can three museums' open data be joined well enough to search as one collection? It takes paintings made 1850–1900 from each museum, joins them in a knowledge graph through Wikidata, and builds a search on top.

The pipeline

  1. Harvest from three APIs. The Rijksmuseum's linked data service, The Met Collection API and the Art Institute of Chicago API. Requests are sequential and spaced out, every response is cached, and the project never writes to any museum.
  2. Convert to Linked Art. Each record becomes Linked Art JSON-LD with its provenance: source, URL, fetch date and licence, and a separate licence on any field that differs.
  3. Match every artist to Wikidata. A museum's direct Wikidata link counts. Otherwise, two independent IDs must agree (for example AIC's artist ID and ULAN), or one ID plus the same birth and death years. Everything else goes to a review list rather than being merged. Each artist page shows how its records were matched.
  4. Resolve concepts. Styles and subjects (AAT terms from the Met and AIC, Iconclass codes from the Rijksmuseum) are resolved to Wikidata items. A few links the project asserts itself, such as landscape (AAT) ≈ landscape (Iconclass), sit in a separate graph.
  5. Build the knowledge graph. Everything is loaded into Oxigraph as RDF, with a named graph per source, one for project alignments and one for inferences. An artist's movement on Wikidata becomes an inferred style, never the museum's claim.
  6. Build the search from the graph. SPARQL queries over the graph produce one document per painting, indexed with MiniSearch. The search runs entirely in your browser.
  7. Publish as a static site. No server: the search index, the graph (run in the browser as WebAssembly) and each painting's Linked Art file are static files.

What it found

The test question was whether “Impressionist landscapes” returns works from all three museums, grouped correctly by artist. So far it returns AIC and the Met, but not the Rijksmuseum, and grouping is correct where an artist appears in several museums.

  • AIC states the style itself, so its Impressionist landscapes match on its own data.
  • The Met has no style field; its works match only through the artist's movement on Wikidata.
  • The Rijksmuseum's painters of the period are mostly the Hague School, which Wikidata keeps apart from Impressionism. The site offers it as a related movement rather than merging the two.

The full reports are in the repository: Impressionist landscapes and the earlier single-artist study, Van Gogh across three museums.
